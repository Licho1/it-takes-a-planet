# It Takes a Planet

People say things like "robots building robots, from ore to finished product". This project wants to show how much is really needed to make a humanoid robot, a car or a phone.

## Motivation

A modern product does not come from one factory. It needs:

- mines and raw materials (for example indium, silicon, potash for fertilizer),
- factories, machines and measuring instruments,
- chemicals and processes (for example the surface treatment of a single screw),
- suppliers that check quality (for example the composition of steel),
- people who design the product and write the software,
- food for these people, and the farms and tractors that produce the food,
- the biosphere that makes all of this possible: plants and other photosynthetic organisms, fungi, soil bacteria, pollinators.

Each of these needs other things again. The chain does not end. It closes into loops: tractors need steel, steel needs workers, workers need food, food needs tractors. In practice, a large part of the whole planet takes part in making one robot.

We want to make this visible.

## What we want to build

- A clickable web app with a globe.
- Click a product (humanoid robot, car, phone). The places involved in making it appear on the globe. Almost the whole planet lights up.
- Go deeper step by step: from the product to its parts, from a part to its materials, machines, people and places.
- Photos of the real places and buildings where possible.
- Where we do not know the exact supplier or place, we estimate. Estimates are clearly marked.
- Neutral tone. The goal is to show the complexity and the connections, not to judge.
- Several languages.
- Open source.

## A related question

How hard is real, full self-replication? What is the smallest closed system that could make a humanoid robot from raw materials, including the machines that make the machines?

## Related work

- Leonard Read, *I, Pencil* (1958): essay about how no single person knows how to make a pencil.
- Thomas Thwaites, *The Toaster Project*: an attempt to make a toaster starting from ore.
- [Anatomy of an AI System](https://anatomyof.ai/): the Amazon Echo as a map of materials, labor and data (static poster).
- [Fairphone source map](https://www.fairphone.com/hub/en/impact/source-map-transparency/): interactive map of the direct suppliers of a phone.
- [Historical Tech Tree](https://www.historicaltechtree.com/): dependencies between technologies over time, without geography.

These show either the first level of suppliers, or a static picture, or dependencies without places. This project follows the chain much deeper, on a map, including the loops and the biosphere.
